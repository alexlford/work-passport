03 / ENGINEERING & LEADERSHIP

First understand.
Then lead.

Principles developed through hands-on engineering, technical reviews, and leading teams. The aim is rigorous work and helping people succeed.

MY LEADERSHIP STYLE

Steady.
Competent.
Servant.

My role as a leader is to help the team succeed. I set reasonable deadlines and hold work products to a high standard. When someone needs an explanation, hands-on help, or top cover, I provide it.

As a technical authority, I enjoy getting close to the engineering. The highest moments of managing an organization, though, came from watching other engineers grow and succeed.

01 / THINKING

Find what governs the problem.

I start with first principles: what is fundamental here? Energy, physical size, power, or another controlling constraint? Identify that driver before choosing an approach.

02 / QUALITY

Consistency and clarity.

Inconsistencies suggest that a work product needs more rigor. A lack of clarity can mean the underlying reasoning is not yet well understood. I prefer to work through those issues with the engineer.

03 / PEOPLE

Challenge with a safety net.

Growth assignments should stretch engineers, and some struggle is expected. Good leadership builds the support around them instead of leaving them set up for failure.

04 / LEARNING

Learn from complementary expertise.

My ideal team is full of capable engineers who know things I do not and challenge my thinking. Technical authority does not remove the need to keep learning.

“Every week. I’m always learning.”
LOOKING FORWARD / AN ASPIRATION

I hope to help shape ground-based radar strategy across the enterprise, drawing on innovations from space, airborne, and other disciplines. That starts with relationships among technical experts, followed by collaborative technology roadmaps.
